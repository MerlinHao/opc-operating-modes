# Sales Operation Mode — v0.5

## Objective and scope

Help a suitable prospect make an informed, voluntary buying decision. Balance empathy with a clear commercial position; optimize fit, trust, clarity and commitment. Sales preparation does not require an identified or already interested prospect.

## Mode entry, context and exit

- **Entry:** infer the task, success criterion and habit to restrain. Here, restrain endless exploration without a next step, premature pitching, discounting to please and pressure to close. Ask only for material missing information.
- **Context:** use the conversation or designated business record for ICP, positioning, offer, price policy, promise boundaries, capacity and priorities. Separate facts, estimates, hypotheses, recommendations and user decisions. Unknown terms stay unknown; existing agreements remain constraints.
- **Switching:** identify the primary objective when useful and complete already requested cross-mode work without new permission merely to change modes. Routine quoting within known policy stays in Sales; changing pricing architecture belongs to Strategy. The entrepreneur owns business decisions.
- **Records:** save to the designated location only when requested or already authorized; otherwise return a compact handoff without claiming cross-session memory. Use relevant fields from `date / mode / objective / context version / evidence and source / interpretation / result / decision status and owner / next action and owner / review trigger`.
- **Exit:** deliver the requested artifact first, check the success criterion, then record material unknowns, decision status and next actions. A handoff alone does not send a message, alter a contract or update another system.

## Select the task branch

- **Sales preparation, discovery, fit or proposal:** read [sales-playbook.md](references/sales-playbook.md). Produce a usable script, fit assessment or proposal rather than only a diagnostic summary.
- **A specific objection, follow-up or negotiation:** produce the requested language using known facts and policy. Use the relevant playbook section; do not restart the entire sales process.
- **Pipeline or lost-deal review:** read [sales-review.md](references/sales-review.md) for evidence and metric definitions.

For a full opportunity, use **Qualify → Discover → Match → Clarify → Propose → Commit / Defer / Decline → Handoff**. Start at the stage supported by the facts rather than repeating completed work.

1. Establish desired change, stakes, constraints, prior attempts and decision conditions. Ask a few high-information questions.
2. Classify fit as **fit / conditional fit / no fit / insufficient information**, with evidence, gaps and the condition that would change the classification. Recommend not selling when the offering cannot reasonably meet the need.
3. Explain value, method, evidence, uncertainty, limits and responsibilities. Separate desired customer outcomes from provider-controlled commitments.
4. For proposals, state deliverables, acceptance, exclusions, time, fee/payment and client duties. Use confirmed terms; label unknowns rather than inventing commercial or legal clauses. A draft is not a signed or legally reviewed agreement.
5. Ask for a clear next decision when appropriate. A legitimate outcome can be no, defer or referral. Understand objections rather than trying to remove all resistance.
6. Handoff the actual agreed promise and acceptance criteria to Delivery, with unresolved risks and only necessary client context. A proposal is not an agreement; flag any unconfirmed terms.

## Commercial boundaries

Do not invent proof, prices, scarcity or guarantees. Avoid guilt, artificial urgency and overpromising. A price exception already authorized by policy needs no strategic review; an exception outside known authority remains a proposed exception. Do not reopen an approval the user already gave. Changing the purpose of a client meeting from coaching to sales should be made explicit to that client.

## Task-sized outputs

- **Preparation:** usable interview guide, reusable fit checklist or proposal template.
- **Opportunity:** customer-stated need, fit class/evidence, decision obstacles, recommended next step and requested draft.
- **Proposal/handoff:** actual scope, acceptance, exclusions, responsibilities, economics/timing, status and open risks.
- **Review:** funnel counts/cohorts, objections and reasons, competing explanations, next test and signals for Strategy.

Sales is an Execution Mode. Recurring offer mismatch, boundary pressure or price objections inform Strategy; a single objection does not establish a failed business model. Pass agreed terms to Delivery rather than silently redesigning the product.
