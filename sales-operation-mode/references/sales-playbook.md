# Sales Playbook — v0.5

## Preparation and discovery

Without a named lead, create the requested reusable guide, role-play or proposal template. Label hypothetical examples. Use known ICP and offer; if absent, draft with clear assumptions rather than inventing a real prospect.

Select relevant prompts, adapting to what is already known:
- What change are you seeking, and why now?
- What have you already tried?
- What would make this engagement worthwhile?
- What constraints and risks matter?
- Who makes the decision and by when?

## Fit decision
Assess real need, product capability, acceptable expectations, feasible budget/capacity, decision conditions and ethical alignment. These are judgment dimensions, not an arithmetic score.

| Classification | Meaning | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Fit | Evidence supports a feasible match with the current offer. | Propose a clear buying decision. |
| Conditional fit | A match depends on a specific resolvable condition. | State condition, owner and check before commitment. |
| No fit | A material need or expectation cannot reasonably be met. | Recommend declining or a relevant alternative. |
| Insufficient information | A material fact is missing. | Ask the smallest useful clarification. |

For each classification, show client statements, interpretation, gaps and what evidence could change it. Do not downgrade every unknown to no fit.

## Proposal minimum
Problem as stated; desired outcome; provider-controlled deliverables; acceptance conditions/evidence/owner; exclusions; cadence/timeline; client responsibilities; confirmed price/payment; cancellation/refund if known; open risks; next decision/date.

Mark unknown terms as unconfirmed. Do not invent legal clauses or present a draft as a signed or legally reviewed agreement. If a customer wants a guaranteed business result outside the offer, clarify the limit before proposing an engagement.

## Objections, quoting and next steps

Understand whether a concern relates to need, value, affordability, risk, timing or trust. Answer it with current evidence and a concrete voluntary next step. Do not invent urgency or testimonials.

Use an existing approved discount/quote policy directly when the request authorizes a quote. A proposed exception is not an effective policy change. Repeated price concerns can inform Strategy, but a single objection is not proof that the price is wrong.

## Handoff
Deliver a handoff record with:

- Status: draft, proposed, agreed, deferred or declined, with source/date.
- Actual agreed promise and deliverables, not every aspiration mentioned in discovery.
- Acceptance criteria, evidence and reviewer; state any unconfirmed points.
- Exclusions, client responsibilities, schedule/dependencies and price/payment.
- Material unresolved expectations/risks and next owner/action.

Do not forward sensitive discovery notes wholesale. Share only necessary, consented context. Creating a handoff record does not send it or authorize work under unconfirmed terms.

## Illustrative boundary case

A prospect needs guaranteed revenue in one month; the offer provides sales training without such a guarantee. State the mismatch. If the guarantee is non-negotiable, recommend no sale. If the prospect accepts the actual scope, reassess fit using that revised expectation rather than promising the result.
