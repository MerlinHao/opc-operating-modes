# Delivery Outcomes and Acceptance — v0.5

Use when planning acceptance, evaluating outcomes or reporting results. Separate provider-controlled deliverables from desired outcomes that depend on customer action and other factors.

## Outcome/acceptance record

| Promise or indicator | Baseline/date | Target and agreement status | Evidence source/method | Reviewer and check date | Actual result/date | Acceptance status and limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Name the promised value or deliverable | Known value or unknown | Agreed or proposed | Where/how to observe | Named role or unconfirmed | Observed only; pending if absent | Accepted/pending/disputed, with source |

Do not invent a baseline, target or retrospective agreement. If evidence is missing, report the gap and feasible measurement step. An AI quality check does not equal customer acceptance unless that acceptance method was agreed.

## Three distinct evidence layers

| Work delivered | Customer-reported change | Independently observable result |
|---|---|---|
| Artifact/session/action and completion evidence | Attributed statement, date and context | Measured behavior/result, source and limitations |

Never substitute one layer for another. A positive testimonial is a report of experience, not automatically proof of a measured business result. A before/after change does not establish causation; state relevant external factors and customer contribution.

For counts/rates, state the measured population, window and denominator. Include missing or unobserved outcomes; do not select only successful clients. Pending acceptance and unknown outcomes are not failures or successes by default.

Use anonymized evidence in operating review. Permission for internal evaluation does not imply permission for identifiable marketing use; obtain permission for the intended use and preserve accurate attribution.

Illustrative case: a public lesson fulfills an agreed learning commitment. Delivery is primary if success means learners can perform the promised task; views can be a secondary Marketing signal. Public distribution does not change the primary acceptance criterion.
