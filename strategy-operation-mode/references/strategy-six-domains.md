# Six Strategy Domains — v0.5

| Domain | Decision | Evidence | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market & Customer | Who to serve | Interviews, lead fit | ICP and exclusions |
| Positioning & Value Proposition | Why choose us | Alternatives, customer language | Positioning hypothesis |
| Product & Offering | What to sell | Requests, delivery data | Offer/scope and roadmap |
| Business Model | How economics work | Revenue, cost, time | Pricing policy, unit economics |
| Resource Allocation | Where to focus | Bottleneck, capacity, actual mode time, opportunity cost | Time/capital priorities; build/buy/automate choices |
| Review & Experimentation | What to learn | Cross-mode feedback | Decision log, experiment |

Six domains are a scope map, **not** a six-section compulsory weekly report. Select the domains needed for the actual decision. Outputs remain recommendations until decided by the user or within explicitly delegated authority.

## Decision depth by domain

- **Market & Customer:** distinguish a hypothesized segment from observed need, willingness to act and actual transactions. Record exclusions and alternatives to the selected market.
- **Positioning & Value Proposition:** compare with customer alternatives, including doing nothing or doing it themselves. A memorable slogan is not validated differentiation.
- **Product & Offering:** define outcomes, provider commitments, exclusions, dependencies and the simplest viable offer. Do not build a portfolio before demand justifies it.
- **Business Model:** name the revenue unit, price structure, direct costs, founder time, fixed-cost treatment and capacity assumption. Distinguish contribution from profit and cash flow. Use observed data where available; mark estimates and unknowns.
- **Resource Allocation:** compare bottleneck evidence with actual time spent across modes. Track mixed-task time once under a declared primary mode or split it explicitly; do not double-count. Consider build/buy/automate using cost, reliability and opportunity cost. Do not prescribe universal time percentages.
- **Review & Experimentation:** connect a hypothesis to evidence, bounded action, interpretation and a revisitable decision. Use [strategy-review-template.md](strategy-review-template.md).

Illustrative imbalance: a founder spends most available time polishing already accepted deliverables while qualified opportunities remain scarce. Investigate whether additional delivery effort improves outcomes enough to justify the opportunity cost; this is evidence to examine, not a personality diagnosis or an automatic instruction to cut quality.
