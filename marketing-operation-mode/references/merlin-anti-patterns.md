# Personalized Marketing Anti-Patterns — v0.5

These are working hypotheses for a creator whose strengths include structural thinking, abstraction, teaching, product thinking, interviewing, coaching, and framework building. Treat them as diagnostic prompts, not personality labels. Use only when observed behavior supports the diagnosis or the user requests this lens; do not apply every pattern to every piece of content.

## 1. Framework Prematurity

### Symptom
A raw observation quickly becomes a multi-layer framework, matrix, taxonomy, model, or slide deck.

### Cost
The creator experiences intellectual satisfaction before the audience has experienced relevance.

### Recovery
Ask in order:
1. Who is experiencing this?
2. What happened to them?
3. Why is that surprising or painful?
4. What do I believe it means?
5. Only then: what framework explains it?

## 2. Completeness Inflation

### Symptom
Every counterexample adds another qualifier to the headline.

### Cost
Accuracy improves marginally while memorability collapses.

### Recovery
Split expression into:
- Headline: useful compression;
- Body: important qualifications;
- Framework: full boundary conditions.

Keep a qualification in the headline only when removing it makes the central claim materially misleading.

## 3. Coach-Mode Leakage

### Symptom
The creator remains curious and neutral when public marketing needs a point of view.

### Cost
The audience receives good questions but cannot tell what the creator stands for or sees differently.

### Recovery
Complete the sentence:
“Based on the cases I have seen so far, my current judgment is…”

Then label uncertainty where appropriate rather than deleting the judgment.

## 4. Teacher-Mode Leakage

### Symptom
Content begins with definitions, history, taxonomy, or comprehensive explanation.

### Cost
The audience must invest attention before discovering why the topic matters.

### Recovery
Begin with a concrete situation, contradiction, decision, or consequence where that helps the audience. For requested teaching or practical search content, clear instruction can lead directly; do not add artificial conflict.

## 5. Researcher-Mode Delay

### Symptom
The creator postpones publishing until the topic feels exhaustively researched.

### Cost
Observation-to-feedback cycle becomes too slow.

### Recovery
Distinguish:
- claims requiring verification;
- provisional interpretations that can be clearly labeled;
- personal observations that can be reported directly.

Publish defensible provisional positions and let market feedback guide deeper research.

## 6. Architecture Without Stakes

### Symptom
The model is elegant but emotionally and commercially inert.

### Recovery
Ask:
- What decision changes if this is true?
- What does the audience lose by ignoring it?
- What becomes possible by understanding it?
- What familiar frustration does this explain?

## 7. Identity Diffusion

### Symptom
Many genuine interests generate unrelated content streams.

### Cost
The audience cannot infer what recurring problem the creator helps solve.

### Recovery
Use the Audience and Authority gates. Connect cross-domain knowledge back to a small number of durable business questions. Breadth should strengthen the point of view, not fragment positioning.

## 8. Artificial Marketing Voice

### Symptom
In an attempt to become more “marketable,” language becomes exaggerated, manipulative, or unlike the creator's actual reasoning.

### Cost
Short-term attention can damage trust and creator consistency.

### Recovery
Increase tension through sharper contrast, specificity, consequence, and concrete cases. Do not manufacture certainty or outrage.

## Mode Reminder

The desired change is not “become a marketing personality.” It is to temporarily operate under different optimization criteria:

**Relevance → Tension → Position → Credibility → Clarity → Depth**

This is a heuristic for insight-led public expression. Credibility constrains all stages. Direct useful answers need not adopt a provocative voice.
